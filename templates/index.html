About

About the Project

Mekong Pulse is an interactive hydrological analytics and AI-assisted interpretation platform developed as a final-year academic project. It integrates station-level data exploration, configurable multi-variable visualisation, statistical analysis, multi-model time-series forecasting, and AI-generated analytical reporting into a single, cohesive research environment — grounded in real monitoring data from the Mekong River Commission network.

The Problem

Transboundary river systems such as the Mekong face an accelerating convergence of pressures: upstream hydropower dam proliferation, deforestation-driven catchment degradation, shifting monsoon patterns attributable to anthropogenic climate change, and increasing agricultural water abstraction across six riparian states. These compounding stressors are altering river discharge regimes, sediment dynamics, and seasonal flood pulse behaviour in ways that directly threaten food security, biodiversity, and the livelihoods of an estimated 60–70 million people.

Despite the existence of long-term hydrological monitoring networks coordinated by the Mekong River Commission (MRC), the analytical accessibility of this data remains limited. Raw station records are rarely accompanied by interactive tools that allow non-specialist researchers, students, or policymakers to explore temporal patterns, compare stations spatially, or generate evidence-based forecasts — creating a significant gap between data availability and practical analytical utility.

Why the Mekong River?

The Mekong River — at approximately 4,900 kilometres the twelfth longest river in the world and the longest in Southeast Asia — was selected as the subject of this project for several compelling academic and applied reasons. It represents one of the most biodiverse freshwater ecosystems on Earth, sustaining the world's largest inland fishery and forming the agricultural and ecological backbone of Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam, and the Yunnan province of China.

Critically, the Mekong is a river system under documented, measurable stress. Peer-reviewed literature has recorded statistically significant changes in discharge variability, sediment load reduction downstream of major dams, and shifts in the timing of the annual flood pulse. This makes it an exceptionally relevant subject for applied hydrological analysis: the data is rich, the stakes are high, and the analytical questions are both scientifically meaningful and socially urgent.

Project Rationale and Motivation

This project was undertaken in response to a perceived need for a unified, accessible, and academically rigorous tool that bridges the gap between raw hydrological data and actionable insight. Existing platforms for Mekong basin data either require specialist GIS software, are restricted to institutional access, or present data without supporting analytical or forecasting infrastructure.

The motivation was to demonstrate that modern web technologies, open-source scientific computing libraries, and AI language models can be combined to produce a platform that is simultaneously presentation-ready, analytically substantive, and approachable for students and early-career researchers. The project also served as an opportunity to apply skills in data engineering, statistical modelling, machine learning, and full-stack development within a real-world environmental informatics context.

The choice to incorporate multiple forecasting models — ranging from classical statistical approaches (Holt-Winters, SARIMA) to ensemble machine learning methods (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) and kernel-based regression (SVR) — reflects a deliberate effort to situate the platform at the intersection of traditional hydrology and contemporary data science practice.

Methodology and Data

Station records were sourced from the Mekong River Commission (MRC) monitoring network, encompassing four primary hydrological variables: rainfall (mm), river discharge (m³/s), water level (m), and total suspended solids (mg/l). Data was preprocessed and structured into a station-category relational model, enabling flexible cross-station and cross-variable querying at the presentation layer without requiring a live database connection.

Forecasting is implemented across six models. Classical methods — Holt-Winters triple exponential smoothing and SARIMA — decompose time series into trend, seasonal, and residual components with fitted confidence intervals. Machine learning methods — Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, Support Vector Regression, and Linear Seasonal decomposition — operate on engineered lag features and Fourier harmonic terms to capture non-linear seasonal dynamics. All models generate 95% confidence intervals from in-sample residuals to communicate forecast uncertainty.

The AI-assisted analysis module uses the Gemini language model to generate structured hydrological reports, interpreting statistical summaries, trend directions, station rankings, and data quality metrics in the context of Mekong basin environmental science.

Analytical Capabilities

  • Single and multi-station temporal timeline comparison
  • Year-over-year and multi-decade trend analysis
  • Flow duration curves and monthly climatology box plots
  • Rolling average smoothing and trend overlays
  • Cumulative departure from long-term mean
  • Anomaly detection with statistical threshold bands
  • Correlation scatter plots across hydrological variables
  • Multi-station temporal heatmaps
  • Six-model time-series forecasting with confidence intervals
  • AI-generated structured analytical reports via Gemini
  • Data quality scoring: coverage, variability, trend, and span
  • Automated key findings generation from summary statistics

Technical Architecture

  • Backend: Python · Flask · Pandas · NumPy
  • Statistical models: statsmodels (Holt-Winters, SARIMA)
  • Machine learning: scikit-learn (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, SVR, Linear Regression)
  • AI analysis: Google Gemini API via google-genai
  • Visualisation: Plotly graph_objs and express
  • Mapping: Leaflet.js · GeoJSON basin overlay · CartoDB tiles
  • Frontend: Vanilla JS · CSS custom properties · Inter typeface
  • Persistence: localStorage for sidebar state and theme preference
  • Data format: CSV-based station and category records

Limitations: Forecasting models assume historical patterns persist and are intended as decision-support tools, not operational predictions. Data quality varies by station; sparse records yield less reliable statistical summaries. The AI analysis module requires a valid Gemini API key and is subject to rate limits on the free tier.
